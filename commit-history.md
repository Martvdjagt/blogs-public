# Introducing the AI Authenticity Protocol

The problem with AI is that it has become **so** hard to distinguish what *is good* from what *looks good*.
If I want to read papers generated by AI, then I can just ask the chat agent. 
I have a simple proposal to address this. It is not foolproof, but it at least creates another hurdle.

Save your papers, your books, your blogs in source code.
And when you are done, share your commit history.

I'm all in favour of leveraging AI. It is like saying you shouldn't us google because there are encyclopedias. But just as google has raised the bar, we now again need to raise the bar. You should be able to commit often but at the same time you also want to be able to protect your IP until you are ready to publish. Keep your repository private (e.g. Martvdjagt/blogs) but create a second mirrored one for the papertrail (e.g. Martvdjagt/blogs-public). When you are ready to publish the paper, update the published.json in your private repo and mirror only those files to the public repo.

Will anybody read your commit history? Not by themselves. But if I have access to your history, I at least have the possibility to analyze it. And it sure is a lot harder to "cheat" if you expose your entire development process. Even with AI. It is a lever for editiorial channels to check for authenticity on anything they publish. It is a lever for the public to check for authenticity on anything they read (see my [critique](theoretical-view-on-something-big.md) on viral paper 'Something big is happening').

The challenge with blog posts is distinguishing what is good from what looks good. I spent 20-30 hours writing this paper and am sharing my commit history to prove the progress I have made. Writing something that looks good is easy. Everybody can do it nowadays. So we raise the bar, and that can only be achieved by spending time. Why?

What can you tell from the history of this paper? 
I knew of course that I would be sharing my history, so I am committing more often. Deliberately adding comments to convey upfront the intent that I have now, but perhaps not detailed out.

It took (will take) time to write this, there is an evolution to the article. The time element is, or should be, difficult to fake. So at minimum it will double the throughput before an agent can reliably fake it.
